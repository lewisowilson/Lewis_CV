About the project

The Adaptive Intradermal Patch is a concept by Lewis Wilson, an independent student innovator.

Lewis researched why people stop allergy immunotherapy and found that many give up because of the time commitment of repeated, supervised clinic visits. The patch is his answer to a narrow question: could a device do part of the watching, so that more of a course could one day happen at home?

The concept is at an early stage: TRL 1 to 2, with no prototype and no clinical or user testing. It has been submitted to Health Innovation Kent Surrey Sussex, with no response yet[25]. Lewis has chosen not to pursue a patent, so the design is published here in full.

This site explains the concept, sources every factual claim, and lets you run the device's logic on simulated patients, including its weaknesses. It is not medical advice and makes no claim that the device would work.

Who this is for. Health innovation networks, academic and industry partners who could test any part of the roadmap, and anyone curious about how a medical device concept is reasoned through before anything is built.

More of Lewis's work is at lewis-wilson.com.
