Simulator

This runs the concept's software on simulated patients, in your browser. Nothing here is a measurement and no health data is collected. The skin model is grounded in published ranges for capillary density, ageing and warming[10, 12]; the scan follows the speckle contrast model used by a handheld imager at the University of Twente[7]; the reaction read-out uses the same 15-minute window as a skin prick test[15], and every simulated patient gets a small flare from the needles themselves, because in a microneedle patch trial the usual local effect was redness at the application site[21]. Everything else is an illustrative design value[25].

Skin model

A simulated 10 x 10 mm patch of skin. Capillary loops cluster into richer and poorer zones; density falls with age and rises when skin warms. All values are generated and illustrative.

Safety against convenience: 10,000 simulated patients

Every gate makes two kinds of mistake. Set it strict and it rarely misses a reaction but stops many people who were fine. Set it loose and the reverse. Drag the point along the curve to choose.

What real studies would have to measure

Every number above is generated. To turn this into evidence, a bench study would compare the handheld scan with a mounted reference on the same skin[7]; a reaction-detection study would record how flare size and timing after a test dose relate to what happens after the full dose; and only clinical studies could set the gate's thresholds. Even then, a 15-minute read cannot see reactions that start later, which is why UK guidance observes patients for 60 minutes after each injection[1].
