Quit nicotine on your terms.

A free, not-for-profit quit app concept for UK students aged 18 and over who vape, use pouches or smoke.

See how it works

Vaping tripled among young adults in two years.

Among 18 to 24-year-olds in England, vaping went from 9% to 29% between May 2021 and May 2023, and overall nicotine use rose from 28% to 35%. Vaping among those who had never smoked rose from 2% to 9%. Source: Tattan-Birch et al. 2024, Lancet Regional Health Europe

The law is moving

  1. 1 June 2025 Single-use vapes banned. Source: GOV.UK, single-use vapes ban
  2. 29 April 2026 The Tobacco and Vapes Act becomes law. Source: UK Parliament, Tobacco and Vapes Act 2026
  3. From June 2027 Vape and pouch advertising banned, with powers over flavours and packaging. Source: WHO FCTC, UK Tobacco and Vapes Act summary

A quit app's first job is to be used.

In a UK trial of 3,143 people, simply offering a leading quit app made no detectable difference to quit rates. The people who actually downloaded it did better. Source: Jackson et al. 2024, JMIR Most apps were also built for cigarettes, not vapes or pouches.

Cravings happen in places and moments you can predict.

The pub on a Friday. A mate's flat. The smoking area between lectures. Exam season, Sunday nights, the walk home. DeNic lets you map those in advance, write a plan in your own words, and hands it back at exactly the right moment.

Nights out matter most: among students who vape, vaping to socialise and vaping while drinking are both linked to more alcohol problems. Source: Ou et al. 2025, J American College Health

The three hero features

The hero features

Risk Places

Drop a pin on the places that get you, set the times they matter, and write an if-then plan. When you arrive, your phone shows your plan, in your words, with a voice note from past you, SOS and one tap to your buddy. Each visit without a slip cools the pin from ember to teal.

Why: in a study of 57 young adults, getting their own if-then plan just before a risky moment cut lapses. Source: Huh et al. 2021, Translational Behavioral Medicine Geofenced support has been shown to be feasible in small UK and US studies. Source: Naughton et al. 2016, JMIR mHealth and uHealth

Night Out Mode

One tap when you head out. Where you're going, your plan, a bail-out buddy and a quiet check-in, set in under 30 seconds. At 11pm: “Still on track? Tap yes, or hit SOS if it's getting hard.” Next morning, a gentle debrief with no judgement either way.

Craving SOS

One big button that gets you through the next three minutes: name the craving, breathe with the circle, then a 60-second game, your reasons, your voice note or a text to your buddy. Then “Craving passed?” and an optional one-tap log.

Why: urge surfing, tested with student smokers, didn't shrink cravings in the moment but people smoked less in the week after. SOS is about riding it out. Source: Bowen and Marlatt 2009, Psych Addictive Behaviors

Everything else, built around student life.

  • Slip, not reset

    A slip changes one day, never wipes your progress. Then: what was going on, and should it become a Risk Place?

  • Risk Times

    Exam season, Sunday evenings, late-night study, the walk home.

  • Quit day or taper

    Step down vape strength or pouch count over weeks. Never back to cigarettes.

  • Quit Pact

    Quit with a mate. Shared milestones and a one-tap “I need a hand”.

  • Pledge

    Optional: put down £10 to £30, get it back at four weeks, or it goes to your charity.

  • Money and health, in student terms

    Savings as nights out and a festival ticket by June, from your own estimates. Health changes only where the NHS publishes them.

  • Trigger insights

    “Most of your cravings are on Friday nights after 10pm”, with one thing to try.

  • Text Companion

    For people who won't open an app: text CRAVE, SLIP or STOP.

  • Get more help

    NHS Stop Smoking Services, pharmacists, GPs and student support, one tap away.

Built on evidence

Each feature borrows from research.

DeNic itself hasn't been tested yet. “Built on evidence” means every feature stands on published findings, not that DeNic has been shown to work.

Risk Places
Q Sense, a UK app, learned people's own smoking locations and sent support on arrival; participants confirmed it found the right places. Source: Naughton et al. 2016, JMIR mHealth and uHealth A small pilot with young adults found geofence-triggered messages technically feasible. Source: Thrul et al. 2025, mHealth Reminders of your own if-then plan before a risky moment cut lapses. Source: Huh et al. 2021, Translational Behavioral Medicine
Text Companion and tone
In a trial of 2,588 18 to 24-year-olds, a quit-vaping text programme gave nearly 40% higher odds of quitting (24.1% vs 18.6%). Source: Graham et al. 2021, JAMA Internal Medicine Its developer reports users were also more likely to stay off both vapes and cigarettes (25.9% vs 18.5%). Source: Truth Initiative
Night Out Mode
Among students who vape, vaping to socialise and vaping while drinking were both linked to more alcohol problems. Source: Ou et al. 2025, J American College Health
Pledge
A 2025 Cochrane review found high-certainty evidence that financial incentives, including putting down your own deposit, help people stay quit, even after the incentive ends. The trials were about smoking; applying it to vapes and pouches is a design choice. Source: Notley et al. 2025, Cochrane
Slip, not reset
In a real-time study of 203 people quitting, a drop in confidence after a slip predicted the next one coming sooner. So DeNic never wipes your progress. Source: Kirchner et al. 2012, J Abnormal Psychology
The whole set
A 2025 review of 16 studies found quit-vaping tools did better when they were personal, tracked behaviour and added social support. Source: Fonteyne et al. 2025, JMIR

Your location never leaves your phone.

  • Geofences are watched on the phone itself. No location is ever uploaded.
  • No account needed. Logs and plans live on the device.
  • No ads, no data sold, not-for-profit. Pacts share milestones only unless you choose more.

This website asks for no location either: the prototype's map is an illustration of a made-up town.

How it would be built

React Native with Expo, so one codebase covers iPhone and Android. Everything that matters runs on the phone; the AWS backend is optional.

Geofencing

Expo Location with TaskManager region monitoring. iOS watches 20 regions per app and Android 100; DeNic caps Risk Places at 15 to leave room for Risk Times. Android can deliver an arrival a couple of minutes late, so plans are written to still make sense then. Source: Expo Location docs

On-device first

SQLite for plans and logs, local notifications fired by region entry and Risk Times, and home and lock-screen widgets for SOS and Night Out.

Optional backend

AWS Amplify Gen 2: Cognito for optional accounts, AppSync and DynamoDB for encrypted backup and Quit Pacts, Lambda for Pledge payments and the Text Companion.

Store compliance

A clear explainer before asking for “Always” location, honest purpose strings and privacy labels, and no medical claims.

What comes next

  1. Smartwatch detection

    Research shows smartwatches can recognise smoking hand gestures. Source: Das et al. 2025, JMIR Whether that works for vaping is an open question, so it's on the roadmap, not in the app.

  2. University partnerships

    Students' unions and wellbeing teams, and freshers' week campaigns.

  3. Research mode

    Opt-in, anonymised data for academic evaluation.

  4. Evaluation

    A pilot first, then a randomised trial against a basic quit app.

About

Designed by Lewis Wilson

Most quit apps were built for cigarettes, count streaks that reset, and know nothing about a Friday night. I wanted to design one around how students actually live: the pub, a mate's flat, exam season.

DeNic is a concept: the research, the product design and this working prototype. It hasn't been built as a real app or tested with users yet, and it would be free and not-for-profit if it were.
