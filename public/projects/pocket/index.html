Why willpower alone doesn't cut it

  • The wait and the choice do the work. In the same research, a message asking you to reflect did nothing on its own S4. So the Pause shows a breath, not a lecture.
  • Solo limits are easy to wave away. Apple's own guide says Screen Time limits can be ignored once reached, by default S9.
  • Generous limits can backfire. A working paper suggests limits set above what you already use can make you use more, while low ones help S8.
  • Limits are hard to keep alone. A 2018 trial capped social media at 30 minutes a day and saw loneliness and depression fall S7. A four-study replication found most people couldn't stick to the cap, and well-being didn't improve S6.
  • Breaks are normal. Over about 13 weeks, people took breaks from the pause and, coming back, "quickly rebound from a pattern of overuse" S5.

So Pocket keeps the pause, keeps limits low and kind, and brings friends in to make it stick. That last part is Pocket's bet, not a proven finding.

Three things Pocket does differently.

The Pause

Open an app you picked and you get a breath first: five seconds, then Open or Not now. Reopen it the same day and the pause grows to 10, 20, then 30 seconds.

No guilt-trip text, because the message on its own doesn't work S4. After Not now there's a two-minute swap if you fancy one. Choose Open and you pick 5, 10 or 15 minutes.

Phone-free places and times

The library, lectures, your bed after midnight and the dinner table get stricter rules on their own: a longer pause, or a block on the apps you choose.

Rules stack, and the stricter one wins. Where you are is worked out on your phone and never leaves it.

Table Mode

Start a session at dinner or the pub. Friends join by QR code or link, agree the stakes if they want any, and phones go down together.

Pick up and the group hears about it: "Nico picked up at 23:14. That's a round." Need your phone for something real? "I need my phone" is a no-shame exit, and calls, maps and emergencies always work.

Keyholder

Hard mode, with a friend holding the key. Lock an app or the whole phone until a time. Leaving early means asking, and they can say yes or "you've got this". Essentials always work.

Low limits, by default

Pocket suggests a daily allowance below what you use now and steps it down each week. A pocket that empties, not a countdown.

Swap, not just block

After Not now, one tap to two minutes of something else: message a real person, stretch, look out of the window. Add your own.

Honest dashboard

Pickups, opens per hour, late-night minutes, and the opens you dropped at the Pause, which count as wins.

Wind-down and wake-up

Phone across the room, a real alarm, and the first 30 minutes of the day phone-free. Late-night opens get a longer pause.

Exam Mode

One tap for revision season: the library goes phone-free, a Study Table with friends, longer pauses, a smaller allowance.

Slip, not reset

Going over never wipes your progress. Switch Pocket off for a few days and it invites you back, gently.

  • Your places and times

  • Your allowance and your week

Shared, only if you use a social feature

  • Table Mode: that you're in the session, and pickups during it

No ads. No data sold. An account only if you use Table Mode or a Keyholder.

How it would be built.

This site is a prototype. The real app would be native on both platforms, because the part that matters most, stepping in before an app opens, needs system APIs S10.

iPhone

Swift and SwiftUI on Apple's Screen Time API. FamilyControls lets you pick the apps, and needs Apple's Family Controls entitlement, which Apple must approve before release. ManagedSettings puts a shield over chosen apps. DeviceActivity runs schedules and usage thresholds.

A shield is a fixed system screen drawn by a sandboxed extension, so it can't animate. From iOS 26.5 its button can open Pocket, which plays the breathing Pause; on older versions the shield itself is the pause, in text. Usage numbers can only be shown through Apple's sandboxed DeviceActivityReport, so they stay on the phone.

Android

Kotlin. UsageStatsManager, with the usage access you grant in Settings, spots when a chosen app comes to the front. The Pause is drawn over the app, which needs the "display over other apps" permission. An Accessibility-based version would need Google Play's declaration and a prominent disclosure.

Rules are checked the moment an app opens, so bed time is exact. WorkManager, which runs at most every 15 minutes, only handles the weekly allowance step-down.

Places, friends, privacy

Native region monitoring: iOS allows 20 regions per app and Android 100 geofences, so Pocket caps at 15. Table Mode and Keyholder run on AWS Amplify Gen 2: Cognito for optional accounts, AppSync real-time subscriptions, DynamoDB and Lambda. Friends join by QR code or link.

Why not React Native? Screen Time shields and Android interception are native either way, so a bridge would only sit in front of the core feature.

This website: a static Next.js site for AWS Amplify Hosting. The prototype is React running a small, tested state machine, saved only in your browser.

Why I built it.

I'm Lewis Wilson. I designed Pocket because most screen time apps feel solo, punitive and built for productivity, while the research points somewhere kinder: a short pause, low limits, and other people.

Pocket is a concept. This site and its prototype show how it would work and how it would be built. It hasn't been tested with users yet, so everything here is built on evidence, not proven by it.
