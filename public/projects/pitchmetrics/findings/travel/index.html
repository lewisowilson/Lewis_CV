Q2c, confirmatory

Travel

Do longer away trips help the home side?

No evidence

What fans assume

A long coach trip from the south coast to the north east is meant to leave the away side flat.

The naive view

Split the 1,520 test matches (2022/23 to 2025/26) into five equal groups by away travel. Home sides in the top group took 1.62 points per match; in the bottom group, 1.47. A gap of +0.15 points, before anything is controlled for.

Controlling for team strength

Now subtract what the strength model (Elo blended with Dixon-Coles) already expected before kick-off. The gap between the top and bottom groups becomes +0.17 points per match.

The pre-registered test puts a number on it: one standard deviation more away travel is worth +0.059 expected points per match (95% interval +0.001 to +0.114).

The market test

The closing market is a tougher control than our model: it has team news and the money of people paid to be right. With the market's probabilities in place of ours the estimate is +0.047 points per match (−0.010 to +0.102). The interval includes zero.

The verdict

No evidence

No evidence after correction. Each extra 110 km of travel for the away side is worth +0.06 points per match to the home side (unadjusted p = 0.05, 0.32 after correction).

Unadjusted p = 0.046; after the Holm correction for testing nine things at once, 0.324. Rule fixed in advance in the pre-registration.

What it means

The closest call of the nine. The estimate points the expected way, but once the test is corrected for the other eight questions, and once the market is the control, it no longer stands out.

Verdict rule: "Real effect" needs a Holm-adjusted p below 0.05 and a practically meaningful size; "Small effect" is detectable but small; "No evidence" means the adjusted p is 0.05 or more. Methodology.
