Side finding

Empty stadiums

Did home advantage leave with the fans?

What fans assume

The crowd is the twelfth man. Take the fans away and home advantage should go with them.

The natural experiment

From June 2020 to the end of 2020/21, 452 Premier League matches were played behind closed doors. Home sides won 39% of them and away sides 38%, against 44% and 32% with crowds in the seasons either side.

Controlling for team strength

The model compares each team's scoring at home and away within the same season, with and without fans. The change in the home scoring advantage is −0.110 on the log scale (95% interval −0.256 to +0.035, p = 0.14).

What it means

Without crowds, home sides scored 1.06 times their away rate, against 1.19 with crowds. The point estimate says about 64% of the home goal advantage went with the fans, but the interval includes no change.

Crowd status is coded by date from published UK government rules: behind closed doors from June 2020, up to 2,000 fans in some areas in December 2020, closed again from January 2021. The last two rounds of 2020/21 (up to 10,000 fans) are left out. A side finding: pre-registered, outside the main family of tests.
