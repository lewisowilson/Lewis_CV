Q3a, confirmatory

Weather and goals

Do temperature, rain or wind change the number of goals?

No evidence

What fans assume

Wet balls skid, wind holds up long passes, and a freezing January night is no place for flowing football. Surely the weather changes how many goals go in.

The naive view

The coldest fifth of test matches averaged 2.95 goals; the warmest fifth, 2.91. The overall average was 2.95.

Controlling for team strength

The pre-registered model takes each match's expected goals from the strength model and asks whether temperature, rain or wind add anything. All three estimates sit within a tenth of a goal of zero, with intervals that include it.

The market test

The market check applies to results, not goal totals, so this question has none. The control here is the strength model's expected goals, fitted only on data from before each match.

The verdict

No evidence

No evidence. The largest weather effect is -0.01 goals per match per standard deviation, against an average of 2.95 goals.

Unadjusted p = 0.982; after the Holm correction for testing nine things at once, 1.000. Rule fixed in advance in the pre-registration.

What it means

No evidence. Sorry, weather pundits. Whatever the weather does to a match, it does not show up in the number of goals once you know who is playing.

Verdict rule: "Real effect" needs a Holm-adjusted p below 0.05 and a practically meaningful size; "Small effect" is detectable but small; "No evidence" means the adjusted p is 0.05 or more. Methodology.
