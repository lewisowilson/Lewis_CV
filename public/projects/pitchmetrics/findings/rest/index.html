Q2a, confirmatory

Rest

Does having more rest than the opponent help?

No evidence

What fans assume

Fixture congestion decides seasons. The side with two extra days on the training ground should have fresher legs.

The naive view

Split the 1,140 test matches (2022/23 to 2024/25) into five equal groups by rest gap. Home sides in the top group took 1.37 points per match; in the bottom group, 1.82. A gap of −0.45 points, before anything is controlled for.

Controlling for team strength

Now subtract what the strength model (Elo blended with Dixon-Coles) already expected before kick-off. The gap between the top and bottom groups becomes −0.02 points per match.

The pre-registered test puts a number on it: one standard deviation more rest gap is worth +0.002 expected points per match (95% interval −0.058 to +0.058).

The market test

The closing market is a tougher control than our model: it has team news and the money of people paid to be right. With the market's probabilities in place of ours the estimate is −0.000 points per match (−0.060 to +0.054). The interval includes zero.

The verdict

No evidence

No evidence. One standard deviation more rest (1.8 days) is worth +0.00 expected points per match.

Unadjusted p = 0.938; after the Holm correction for testing nine things at once, 1.000. Rule fixed in advance in the pre-registration.

What it means

Whatever an extra day or two of rest is worth, it leaves no mark that three seasons of complete fixture data can detect once team strength is known.

Verdict rule: "Real effect" needs a Holm-adjusted p below 0.05 and a practically meaningful size; "Small effect" is detectable but small; "No evidence" means the adjusted p is 0.05 or more. Methodology.
