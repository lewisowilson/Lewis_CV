Methodology and assumptions

Structure

Mandate is priced with a collective risk model. For each peril kkk (misinterpretation, price error, duplication, manipulation, counterfeit merchant or invoice fraud, and scope creep), losses arrive as a Poisson process with rate λk\lambda_kλk​ and each loss has a random size XkX_kXk​. The insurer's expected annual cost is

E[Payout]=∑kλk E[g(Xk)]\mathbb{E}[\text{Payout}] = \sum_{k} \lambda_k \, \mathbb{E}\big[g(X_k)\big]E[Payout]=k∑​λk​E[g(Xk​)]

where ggg is the payout function after recoveries, excess, co-payment, limits and shared fault. Perils are independent in the base model; the systemic shock below relaxes that.

Payout and recoveries

For a single loss xxx with excess ddd, per-claim limit LcL_cLc​, co-payment γ\gammaγ and shared fault ϕ\phiϕ:

rrr is the recovery probability for the payment rail and peril class (chargebacks, section 75, APP reimbursement) and eee = 0.85 is recovery efficiency. ϕ\phiϕ is 0 with a signed mandate or full logs and 0.25 if the policyholder dismissed a confirmation prompt. E[g(X)]\mathbb{E}[g(X)]E[g(X)] is computed in closed form where possible and otherwise by quadrature on 2,000 points; tests compare it with simulation within 1%.

The annual aggregate payout is capped at LyL_yLy​. The analytic model applies the cap with Panjer's recursion on a discretised claim distribution.

SSS is the annual net cost and YkY_kYk​ the net cost of one claim. Illustrative values: α\alphaα = 0.1 (personal) and 0.15 (business); fixed expenses £12 and £120 a year; β\betaβ = 0.25; minimum premiums £24 and £250.

Simulation

The calculator simulates 10,000 policy years in a Web Worker with a seeded xoshiro128** generator. Each year draws Nk∼Poisson(λk)N_k \sim \text{Poisson}(\lambda_k)Nk​∼Poisson(λk​) claims per peril, draws severities, applies recoveries as Bernoulli(rrr) events, then the payout function and annual limit.

Systemic shock

With probability qqq = 0.02 a year, a systemic event multiplies misinterpretation and price-error frequency by sss = 10 for ddd = 7 days, so the annual frequency in a shock year is

λkshock=λk(1+(s−1) d365)\lambda_k^{\text{shock}} = \lambda_k \left(1 + (s - 1)\,\frac{d}{365}\right)λkshock​=λk​(1+(s−1)365d​)

Given the shock state, policies are independent, so the total for a 10,000-policy portfolio is close to normal and the portfolio loss is a two-part mixture. Capital is the 99.5% value at risk minus the expected loss.
